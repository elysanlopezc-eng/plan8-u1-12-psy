CNB Competence & Achievement

Competence: Integrates psychological mechanisms of consumer behavior to design ethical marketing strategies while distinguishing persuasion from manipulation.

Learning Outcome: Evaluates commercial campaigns using cognitive drivers (perception, attention, motivation, learning, social influence) and defends ethical boundary standards.

Key Competencies & Montano Habits

Skills: Ethical Reasoning, Peer Assessment, Campaign Synthesis, Analytical Communication.

Montano Habits: Think Win-Win (balancing commercial impact with consumer trust) & Sharpen the Saw (refining Activity 3 and organizing portfolio evidence).

Resources & Assessment

Resources: Sample Ad Case Cards, Ethical Persuasion Checklist, Activity 3 Portfolio Folder, Interactive Plenary Rubric.

3 Assessment Moments: Ad Analysis Hook (Initial), 3-Concept Campaign Audit (Formative), Peer Feedback & Portfolio Check (Summative).

Stage I: Analyze an Advertisement (~5 Minutes)

Warm-up Prompt: "Look at this fitness brand advertisement: 'Lose 20 lbs in 7 days guaranteed without exercise or diet changes!' Is this persuasive marketing or unethical manipulation?"

Case Sample

"Miracle Fit 7-Day Secret"

Includes countdown timer ("Only 3 bottles left in stock!"), fake doctor testimonials, and photoshopped before/after images.

Diagnosis: Misleading Manipulation

  • Manipulated Mechanisms: Scarcity bias (fake timer), false social proof (fake doctor), unrealistic outcome expectations.
  • Ethical Breach: Deceives consumer understanding, exploits cognitive vulnerabilities, and violates truth-in-advertising principles.

Stage II: Direct Instruction - Concepts & Ethical Boundaries (~30 Minutes)

15 min Identifying 7 Psychological Concepts in Ads | 15 min Persuasion vs. Manipulation & Misleading Claims

1. Perception Sensory interpretation
2. Attention Visual focal cues
3. Motivation Needs & drives
4. Learning Reinforcement habits
5. Attitudes Brand evaluations
6. Social Influence Norms & social proof
7. Decision-Making Heuristics & choices

Ethical Persuasion

  • Provides honest, transparent product information.
  • Helps consumers make informed decisions aligning with their true needs.
  • Respects consumer autonomy and free will.

Psychological Manipulation

  • Uses deceit, hidden terms, or manufactured panic/fear.
  • Exploits cognitive biases (e.g., dark UX patterns, artificial scarcity).
  • Prioritizes short-term sales over consumer well-being.

Stage III: Teams Analyze a Campaign (~15 Minutes)

Teams of 4 select an assigned commercial campaign and audit it using at least THREE psychological concepts.

Team Audit Worksheet

Campaign Element Psychological Concept Identified Mechanism Explanation Ethical Status
Element 1: Visual Contrast & Colors Attention / Perception Directs visual focus to call-to-action button using high contrast colors. Ethical Persuasion
Element 2: "9 out of 10 Dentists Agree" Social Influence / Norms Triggers authority bias to build trust and reduce perceived risk. Ethical Persuasion (if data is verified)
Element 3: Hidden Auto-Renewal Subscription Decision-Making / Dark Pattern Exploits default bias and limited attention to lock consumers into recurring fees. Unethical Manipulation

Stage IV: Peer Feedback & Portfolio Evidence (~10 Minutes)

5 min Peer Feedback Exchange + 5 min Revise Activity 3 & Organize Portfolio Evidence

Peer Feedback Protocol (5 min)

Pair up with another team. Provide 1 praise point on their psychological concept identification and 1 suggestion on their ethical classification.

Portfolio Evidence Checkpoint (5 min)

Finalize revisions on Activity 3 (Ad Campaign Stereotype & Ethical Redesign) and archive all Unit 1 evidence in your digital portfolio.

5-Minute Exit Ticket Prompt

Write 1 sentence: "What is the single most clear boundary line that separates ethical marketing persuasion from deceptive psychological manipulation?"
